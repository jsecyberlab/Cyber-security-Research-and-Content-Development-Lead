L01: Research Notes - What Is Ethical Hacking

Definitions I'm Working With


o	Ethical Hacking:
The authorised process of testing a computer system, network, application or other technology to identify security weaknesses before a malicious attacker can exploit them.

The important word here is authorised.

o	Vulnerability:
any weakness within an organization’s information systems, internal controls, or system processes that cybercriminals or bad actors can exploit.

o	Exploit:
In cybersecurity are chunks of code or software that leverage a flaw or a vulnerability in a computer system to get access to confidential data with the intent of performing malicious actions OR A piece of software, technique, or process that takes advantage of a vulnerability.

	The Six Stages of Ethical Hacking
1. Permission
Written authorisation is essential. It defines scope, rules of engagement, and what you can and can't do. Without permission, it's not ethical hacking.
2. Reconnaissance
Information gathering. This can be passive (just looking at public data) or active (interacting with the target). Examples: domains, subdomains, public information, technologies in use.
3. Scanning & Enumeration
Identifying live hosts, open ports, running services. Tools like Nmap are used here.
 4. Gaining Access
Controlled attempts to validate whether a vulnerability can actually be exploited.
5. Post-Access / Maintaining Access
Privilege escalation, lateral movement (within scope). Assessing potential impact.

6. Reporting
Documenting what was found, evidence supporting the finding, impact, and remediation.
